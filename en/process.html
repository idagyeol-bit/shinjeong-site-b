How We Work

Work starts with a site review and ends with handling the recovered material. In hazardous sections the robot goes in first, and workers enter only after the hazardous material has been reduced.

  1. Home
  2. How We Work

Seven Steps of the Work

STEP 01

Site review & preliminary test

We review whether the method can be applied at the site and run a preliminary test. We check the access openings, internal structure, residue properties and temperature.

STEP 02

System setup

We position the control vehicle and vacuum truck and connect the robot by hose. CCTV and the remote control unit are installed.

STEP 03

First washing

Using the vacuum truck, we first remove residual material from outside the equipment.

STEP 04

Robot entry · internal cleaning

The robot goes in and recovers the remaining hazardous material, then washes the interior with its front jet nozzle (second washing).

STEP 05

Remote monitoring

The operator controls the robot from the control vehicle outside the equipment, watching the interior on CCTV.

STEP 06

Final cleaning

Once the hazardous material has been reduced, workers enter and remove the final residue inside.

STEP 07

Recovered material & waste handling

Recovered material is dewatered with a decanter or filter press, which reduces the amount of waste and shortens the treatment period.

Confined-Space Work: Risk Comparison

A worker who enters the equipment is exposed to the five hazards below. Sending the robot in first avoids them.

Main hazardWith the robot sent in first
Suffocation by toxic gasHazardous material removed beforehand
Contact with high-risk substancesHazardous material removed beforehand
Suffocation from loss of air supplyWorkers kept safe
Tripping on lines and hosesTime inside kept to a minimum
Musculoskeletal injury in tight spacesRobot goes into tight spaces instead

The robot does not replace all of the work. Once the hazardous material has been reduced, workers do the final cleaning.

Applications: Scope, Benefits and Limits

The scope, benefits and limits of the four main applications of our unmanned equipment.

Tank / Pond Cleaning

Scope
  • Work in explosion-proof zones (high-risk gas equipment) — hydraulic drive, rubber tracks, non-spark materials on crushing and contact parts
  • Underwater work, with water-jet cleaning at the same time
  • Extra equipment such as CCTV and jets can be fitted
Benefits
  • Lower risk of casualties from suffocation, burial, entrapment and falls
  • Continuous work through operator shifts and non-stop equipment operation
Limits
  • Final cleaning by workers is still needed once the hazard is reduced

Pond Cleaning (Loader)

Scope
  • Sediment at the bottom of open ditches and ponds
  • Sediment beneath operating equipment
  • Opening an access route into contaminated areas
  • Removal of hardened sludge that is difficult to remove by hand
Benefits
  • Continuous work is possible
  • Workers kept safe from physical, musculoskeletal and fall hazards
Limits
  • Working time limited by battery replacement and charging
  • For use in non-explosion-proof zones only

Jet Cleaning

Scope
  • Water-jet cleaning in tight spaces
  • Unmanned decontamination of contaminated areas (removal of harmful substances)
  • Inspection of tight spaces
Benefits
  • Continuous work is possible
  • Unmanned work in tight spaces
Limits
  • In complex interiors, obstructions may limit cleaning to part of the space
  • Spray from the washing water reduces visibility — compensated by CCTV

Waste (Packing Media) Removal

Scope
  • Removal of packing media inside reactors and tanks, including under a nitrogen atmosphere
  • Work in explosion-proof zones
  • Removal of sludge and solid waste from other equipment
Benefits
  • Workers kept safe from suffocation and exposure to hazardous material
  • Unmanned work in nitrogen-filled spaces
Limits
  • Airborne particles reduce visibility — compensated by CCTV
  • Manway limit: 18 inches minimum

Dewatering of Recovered Material

Dewatering with a decanter (centrifugal separation) or a filter press (filtration and pressing) reduces the amount of waste to be treated, and the cost and time of treating it.

Decanter (Screw Decanter)

Solid–liquid separation by centrifugal force

  1. Feed in
  2. Wash water in
  3. Centrifugal separation
  4. Cake discharged
  5. Separated liquid discharged

Filter Press

Dewatering by filtration and pressing

  1. Feed in
  2. Filter plates pressed
  3. Filtrate discharged
  4. Cake removed
  5. Filter plates washed

Throughput, moisture content and other specifications depend on how the material flocculates, so we provide them during consultation.
